# Develop a fresh idea from the actual problem

An idea should make this experience more useful or more specific. Being unusual is not enough. Do not add a new visual effect merely to prove creativity.

## Start from a tension

Identify something the visitor must reconcile: atmosphere versus practical information; an attractive object versus its everyday use; a powerful tool versus uncertainty about its operation; many events versus finding the right time; or a story versus the need to scan quickly.

The tension creates an opportunity. It does not prescribe a component.

## Generate through different lenses

| Lens | Question | Possible consequence, not a required pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence | What can the real content prove? | Lead with actual work or an operation instead of a claim |
| Relationship | What information belongs together? | Connect an object to its room or a programme to its date |
| Sequence | What must be understood first? | Reorder the narrative before changing visual styling |
| Decision | What uncertainty prevents action? | A useful comparison or clearly stated limit |
| Material | What does the subject look/feel/sound like? | Specific imagery, language or type treatment supported by assets |
| Constraint | What limits force a better solution? | A concise type-led composition when photography is weak |
| Interaction | What work can the interface remove? | A real filter, selection or preparation step |
| Restraint | What can be removed? | A stronger page with fewer competing ideas |

Use more than one lens. Do not repeat one signature idea across unrelated projects.

## Separate originality from imitation

Extract a reference's mechanism, then restate it without its branded visual form.

For example, “product behavior supplies evidence” can become a small operation suited to the actual tool. It should not become a copy of another company's browser frame, color, copy or animation.

“A place is connected to what happens there” can support a practical cafe visit path or a cultural programme. It does not mean every place needs a cinematic carousel.

## Write an idea card

- The visitor problem and subject-specific idea.
- Evidence or research that inspired it.
- What the visitor gains.
- Required assets and facts.
- The smallest robust implementation.
- Accessibility, performance and maintenance cost.
- How to tell whether it improved the result.
- Accept, test, defer or reject, with a reason.

## Challenge before polishing

Ask whether the same benefit is available with a simpler arrangement. Ask whether the idea still works on a short phone, with reduced motion, with longer text and without perfect assets.

If a familiar control is the best answer, use it. Originality can be in the content relationship and composition rather than a custom interaction.

## Keep a live backlog

Record promising alternatives during research and critique. Revisit them when content, evidence or visitor needs change.

Do not implement every idea. Choose a coherent main direction and a few supporting decisions. Too many individually attractive ideas can make the page less distinctive and harder to use.

Fresh research and a clear problem should keep generating options. The review process determines which options earn a place.
