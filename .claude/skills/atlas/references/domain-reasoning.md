# Domain reasoning without domain templates

Use this to ask better questions. Do not turn a row into a palette, font pair or section recipe.

## Hospitality, cafes and places

Visitor questions: What is it like? What can I get? Where is it? When can I visit? What action is available now?

Useful evidence: actual food/space photography, menu, hours, location, accessibility/facility details and truthful operational information.

Potential interactions: menu filtering, directions, a clear contact path, a useful gallery or an authorized reservation flow.

Common failure: borrowing SaaS charts, excessive atmospheric scrolling before practical details, invented opening status, or a fake booking system.

A small local cafe may need a concise practical page rather than a resort-like cinematic journey.

## Furniture, objects and commerce

Visitor questions: What is the object? Will it fit? What is it made of? How does it look in context? What are the real purchase constraints?

Useful evidence: object views, scale, materials, dimensions, variants, context and actual pricing/availability where supplied.

Potential interactions: genuine variation, comparison, filtering or a useful fit aid.

Common failure: identical rounded cards, fabricated craftsmanship, stock imagery represented as inventory, or an atmospheric hero that never reaches specifications.

A room scene, isolated product sheet or material study can all fit; the offer determines which.

## Software and developer tools

Visitor questions: What task does it solve? How does it work? Is it compatible? What are its real limits and cost?

Useful evidence: actual UI, input/output, workflow, documentation, integration requirements and honest constraints.

Potential interactions: a small real demonstration, code copying, scenario comparison or a transparent calculator based on actual rules.

Common failure: a fake terminal, invented metrics, decorative topology, or a simulation whose output is unrelated to input.

A product screenshot is not automatically an interactive demo and should not pretend to be.

## Cultural institutions, events and editorial work

Visitor questions: What is happening? When/where? Who is involved? How do I participate or find related work?

Useful evidence: programme, work, author/artist context, date, venue and accessible practical details.

Potential interactions: date/category filtering, a useful archive, schedule or searchable content.

Common failure: unconventional navigation hiding essential information, tiny decorative labels or motion that makes the programme difficult to scan.

Expressive identity can coexist with very conventional clear controls.

## Education

Visitor questions: What will I learn? Who is this for? What evidence supports quality? What are the application or participation steps?

Useful evidence: actual curriculum, learning environment, staff information, dates and verified outcomes where available.

Potential interactions: curriculum explorer, eligibility guidance or a real application route within scope.

Common failure: fictional student outcomes, generic campus stock presented as the school, or an invented seat/booking system.

## Professional and local services

Visitor questions: Can you solve my specific problem? What is included? What does the process require? How do I contact you?

Useful evidence: actual services, work/process, team credentials where verified, scope, location and real contact information.

Potential interactions: service comparison, inquiry preparation or a concise relevant FAQ.

Common failure: vague value claims, fabricated testimonials, overcomplicated lead funnels or inappropriate consumer-product styling.

## Health-related services

Prioritize accurate service/visit information, credentials supplied by the organization, accessibility and a clear legitimate contact path.

Do not invent clinical claims, advice, guarantees, patient stories or credentials. Separate interface design from medical guidance.

A calm palette is not a substitute for trustworthy content or readable navigation.

## Operational tools and dashboards

Visitor job: see state, prioritize, act accurately and recover.

Useful evidence: actual task/data model, ownership, status, permissions and failure modes.

Potential interactions: filtering, editing, reviewing, bulk action only where authorized, confirmation and recovery where appropriate.

Common failure: applying landing-page whitespace, decorative animation, tiny controls, fake analytics or unnecessary card surfaces to dense work.

The correct design may be quieter and denser than a marketing page.

## Cross-domain discipline

For every project answer the same final questions:

- What must a visitor know before acting?
- What truthful evidence exists?
- What should be experienced first?
- What is the simplest useful interaction?
- Which attractive reference idea does not transfer?
- What does mobile require changing?
- Which implementation cost is justified by actual value?

Let those answers select composition, type, color, geometry and behavior.
